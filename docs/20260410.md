## 0. Vision

### The Thesis (formulated before knowing transformer internals)

These models are called **Large Language Models**. The hypothesis: they are large *because of* language, not *because of* reasoning. Reasoning emerged as a byproduct of next-token prediction across diverse multilingual corpora. If we can isolate the reasoning core $f^*$ from the language wrappers $h, h'$ in the factorization $\mathcal{M} = h' \circ f^* \circ h$, then:

1. **$f^*$ should be dramatically smaller than $\mathcal{M}$** — most parameters serve language encoding/decoding, not reasoning
2. **$f^*$ should be trainable alone** — a small, language-free reasoning engine
3. **Larger models reason better because $f^*$ grows richer** — more non-trivial eigenvalues/eigenvectors in the composed dynamics of $f^*$, enabling resolution of problems whose outcome is genuinely uncertain at the start of computation

### The Two Money Shots

**Money Shot 1 (Compression)**: Strip $h$ and $h'$. Train $f^*$ alone. The parameter count should collapse because language — vocabulary embeddings, language-specific routing, output formatting — constitutes the bulk of what makes these models "large." The reasoning core is small, canonical, and potentially language-agnostic by construction.

**Money Shot 2 (Geometry of Reasoning)**: $f^*$ has a canonical Euclidean geometry. Its dynamics are characterized by eigenvalues of the composed layer maps $g_L \circ \cdots \circ g_1$. Larger models permit more non-trivial Lyapunov exponents — directions in state space where the computation genuinely *stretches* or *compresses* information. Small models are "sycophantic" (always predict the obvious next word) because their $f^*$ is too low-rank to sustain the dynamical richness needed for genuine deliberation. The number of non-trivial eigenvalues of $f^*$ may be a direct measure of reasoning *capability*.

### What Our Experiments Bear On This

The experimental program probes the structure of $\mathcal{M} = h' \circ f^* \circ h$ from multiple angles:
- **Is the factorization real?** (F1: PC0 swap = perfect language switch, cos($f_{\text{EN}}, f_{\text{ZH}}$) = 0.952)
- **Is $h/h'$ thin?** (Language = 1 direction at L26, 5–10 dims at L32; flip is efficiency not capability)
- **Is $f^*$ operating in a compressed space?** (Read head uses $\leq 8$ dims; context uses all $d$)
- **Does $f^*$ scale with model capacity?** ($\mathcal{M}_{8B}$ has wider cooperative bandwidth, 10/20 positive modes vs 3–5/20)
- **Is the readout a structural feature of the trained weights?** ($\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$)
- **Can you retrofit the factorization?** (BO: NO — Z-bottleneck catastrophic at all $k$. Must be trained from scratch.)

The low-rank read head, the spectral concentration in weights, the context-only computation — these are all *evidence* for the thesis that $f^*$ is small and separable. They are not the thesis itself. The thesis is: **get rid of the language; you've got reasoning.** And that reasoning engine should be far smaller than the language model that accidentally learned it.

### What We Have So Far

A constructive proof that:
- The $h$-$f^*$-$h'$ factorization holds interventionally (F1, cos = 0.952)
- The readout layer is spectrally encoded in weights alone (C5: $\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$)
- The output channel uses $r \leq 8$ dimensions (C3: lossless compression)
- Context tokens are computationally essential in full $d$ (C2b: single-layer truncation → 0/20)
- Language is thin, distributed, and *efficiency* not *capability* (F2, F3, F4)
- This holds across 4 models, 1.5B–14B, tied and untied embeddings (U1–U5)
- Retrofitting the factorization into existing weights fails (W5/BO) — it must be trained in

What we DON'T yet have: a standalone $f^*$ module. We can observe it, measure it, intervene on it — but we haven't extracted it into a runnable artifact. That's the gap between "evidence that $f^*$ is small" and "here is $f^*$, it's small, and it works."

## 1. Training Context (Givens)

All models studied are autoregressive transformers trained by:

$$\theta^* = \arg\min_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\theta(x_t \mid x_{<t}) \right]$$

via SGD (or Adam/AdamW) on a dataset $\mathcal{D}$ with the following assumed properties:
- **Multilingual**: Chinese and English both well-represented (Qwen family trains on both)
- **Math-inclusive**: Arithmetic, algebra, geometry present in pretraining
- **Next-token objective**: No explicit reasoning supervision; multi-step emerges from serial token prediction
- **No chain-of-thought enforcement**: The model learns to use output tokens as scratchpad *implicitly*

**Key constraint on learned representations**: The output at every position $t$ must predict a *token* — an element of a discrete vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. This forces the final hidden state $h_T^{(L)}$ to be decodable by the unembedding matrix into vocabulary logits. The model has no other output channel.

## 2. Notation

### 2.1 The Model as Composed Map

An autoregressive transformer with $L$ layers maps an input sequence to a probability distribution over the next token:

$$\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{V}^T \to \Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|-1}$$

Decomposed as:

$$\mathcal{M}(x) = \text{softmax}\!\left(W_U \cdot \text{LN}(h_T^{(L)})\right)$$

where $h_T^{(L)}$ is the hidden state at position $T$ (last token) after all $L$ layers.

### 2.2 Input Encoding

$$h_t^{(0)} = W_E[x_t] + \text{PE}(t)$$

- $W_E \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}| \times d}$ — embedding matrix (row-indexed by token ID)
- $\text{PE}(t)$ — positional encoding. For RoPE models: applied inside attention, not added to embeddings. Write $\text{RoPE}(q, t)$ for the rotation applied to query $q$ at position $t$.
- $h_t^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ — input embedding for position $t$

### 2.3 Transformer Layer $\ell \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$

Each layer decomposes into two sublayers with residual connections:

$$\tilde{h}_t^{(\ell)} = h_t^{(\ell-1)} + \text{Attn}^{(\ell)}\!\left(\text{LN}(h_t^{(\ell-1)})\right)$$
$$h_t^{(\ell)} = \tilde{h}_t^{(\ell)} + \text{MLP}^{(\ell)}\!\left(\text{LN}(\tilde{h}_t^{(\ell)})\right)$$

where $\text{LN}$ is RMSNorm (no bias, no centering):

$$\text{LN}(x) = \frac{x}{\|x\|_{\text{RMS}}} \odot \gamma, \quad \|x\|_{\text{RMS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_i x_i^2}$$

### 2.4 Multi-Head Attention with Grouped Query Attention (GQA)

For $n_q$ query heads and $n_{kv}$ key-value heads ($n_q / n_{kv} = G$, the group size):

$$Q_t^{(i)} = W_Q^{(i)} \cdot \text{LN}(h_t^{(\ell-1)}), \quad i \in \{1, \ldots, n_q\}$$
$$K_t^{(j)} = W_K^{(j)} \cdot \text{LN}(h_t^{(\ell-1)}), \quad j \in \{1, \ldots, n_{kv}\}$$
$$V_t^{(j)} = W_V^{(j)} \cdot \text{LN}(h_t^{(\ell-1)}), \quad j \in \{1, \ldots, n_{kv}\}$$

Head $i$ attends using KV group $j = \lceil i/G \rceil$:

$$a_{t,s}^{(i)} = \frac{\exp\!\left(\text{RoPE}(Q_t^{(i)}, t)^\top \text{RoPE}(K_s^{(j)}, s) / \sqrt{d_h}\right)}{\sum_{s' \leq t} \exp(\cdots)}, \quad s \leq t$$

$$\text{head}_t^{(i)} = \sum_{s \leq t} a_{t,s}^{(i)} \cdot V_s^{(j)}$$

Output projection concatenates all heads:

$$\text{Attn}^{(\ell)}(h_t) = W_O^{(\ell)} \cdot [\text{head}_t^{(1)}; \ldots; \text{head}_t^{(n_q)}]$$

**The OV circuit** for head $i$ (the "what information flows" matrix):

$$\text{OV}^{(\ell, i)} = W_O^{(\ell)}_{[i]} \cdot W_V^{(j)}$$

where $W_O^{(\ell)}_{[i]}$ denotes the rows of $W_O$ corresponding to head $i$'s output slice.

The **full-layer OV** (summed over heads, used in C5):

$$\text{OV}_\ell = W_O^{(\ell)} \cdot W_V^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$$

(treating $W_V$ as the block-diagonal expansion under GQA)

### 2.5 MLP (SwiGLU)

$$\text{MLP}^{(\ell)}(x) = W_{\text{down}}^{(\ell)} \cdot \left[\text{SiLU}(W_{\text{gate}}^{(\ell)} x) \odot (W_{\text{up}}^{(\ell)} x)\right]$$

- $W_{\text{gate}}, W_{\text{up}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{ff}} \times d}$
- $W_{\text{down}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_{\text{ff}}}$
- $\text{SiLU}(z) = z \cdot \sigma(z)$

**MLP delta** (the residual-stream contribution of the MLP at layer $\ell$, position $t$):

$$\delta_t^{(\ell)} := \text{MLP}^{(\ell)}\!\left(\text{LN}(\tilde{h}_t^{(\ell)})\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d$$

### 2.6 Output / Unembedding

$$\text{logits}_T = W_U \cdot \text{LN}(h_T^{(L)}) \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$$

- **Tied embeddings**: $W_U = W_E$ (same matrix, transposed). Models: Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B.
- **Untied embeddings**: $W_U \neq W_E$ (separately learned). Models: Qwen2.5-14B, Qwen3-8B.

### 2.7 Autoregressive Generation

At inference (generation of token $\tau$ in the output sequence):
1. Run full forward pass on all positions $1, \ldots, T + \tau - 1$
2. Take $h_{T+\tau-1}^{(L)}$ (the **last token**'s hidden state after all layers)
3. Decode: $x_{T+\tau} \sim \text{softmax}(W_U \cdot \text{LN}(h_{T+\tau-1}^{(L)}))$
4. Append $x_{T+\tau}$ to sequence, repeat

**Key structural fact**: During generation, the KV cache stores $K_s^{(j,\ell)}, V_s^{(j,\ell)}$ for all prior positions $s < T + \tau$. Only the **last token** computes fresh Q and receives a new hidden state update. All prior positions are frozen in the cache.

### 2.8 Subspace Notation

For hidden state $h \in \mathbb{R}^d$, define:
- **$V_\parallel$**: projection onto a subspace $V$ (e.g., top-$k$ SVD directions). $h_{V_\parallel} = P_V h$ where $P_V = U_k U_k^\top$.
- **$V_\perp$**: projection onto the orthogonal complement. $h_{V_\perp} = (I - P_V) h$.

**Gram matrix** of a set of hidden states $\{h_1, \ldots, h_N\} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$G_{ij} = \frac{h_i^\top h_j}{\|h_i\| \|h_j\|} \quad \text{(cosine Gram)}$$
$$\bar{G}_{ij} = \frac{(h_i - \bar{h})^\top (h_j - \bar{h})}{\|h_i - \bar{h}\| \|h_j - \bar{h}\|} \quad \text{(centered Gram, } \bar{h} = \frac{1}{N}\sum h_i\text{)}$$

**Effective rank at threshold $\alpha$**: Given singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots$ of $G$ (or $\bar{G}$):

$$r_\alpha = \min\left\{k : \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N \sigma_i^2} \geq \alpha\right\}$$

### 2.9 Accuracy Metric

All interventional experiments measure math accuracy at $N=20$ problems (5 categories × 4 problems, stratified):

$$\text{Acc} = \frac{|\{i : \text{extract}(\mathcal{M}_\theta(x_i)) = y_i\}|}{N}$$

where $\text{extract}$ parses the final numerical answer from generated text using regex, and $y_i$ is the ground-truth answer. Reported as $k/20$.

## 5. Experiments

### Cluster G: Geometry of the Representation Space

---

#### G1. Cosine Gram Rank Trajectory [Exp BQ]

**Context**: If multi-step reasoning concentrates the residual stream into a subspace, the Gram matrix of hidden states across problems should have low effective rank.

**Procedure**:
1. Run $N=20$ problems (EN + ZH, so 40 sequences) through $\mathcal{M}_{3B}$
2. Extract last-token hidden states $h_T^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}$ at every layer $\ell \in \{0, \ldots, 35\}$
3. Compute cosine Gram matrix $G^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{40 \times 40}$
4. SVD of $G^{(\ell)}$. Report $r_{50}^{(\ell)}, r_{90}^{(\ell)}$

**Result**: $r_{50} = 1$ at ALL layers. The dominant singular value captures >50% of Gram variance everywhere.

**Interpretation**: Trivially expected — Qwen embeddings are highly anisotropic (mean cosine > 0.5), so a rank-1 "everyone points roughly the same direction" component dominates. **Not a structural finding**. The centered Gram is the real object.

---

#### G2. Centered Gram Rank Trajectory [Exp BQ, BQ2]

**Context**: Remove the mean (anisotropy) and ask: what is the intrinsic dimensionality of the *deviations* from the mean?

**Procedure**: Same as G1 but use centered Gram $\bar{G}^{(\ell)}$.

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$):

| Layer range | $r_{50}$ | $r_{90}$ | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------|----------|----------------|
| L0–L5 | 2–3 | 8–10 | Embedding geometry |
| L6–L9 | 3–4 | 12–19 | Build-up (increasing dimensionality) |
| L10–L20 | 3–4 | 18–21 | Equilibrium plateau |
| L21–L32 | 3 | 15–20 | Sustained |
| L33–L35 | 2 | 6–10 | **Reconcentration / canyon** |

**Interpretation**: The representation expands into ~20 effective dimensions during mid-network computation, then sharply reconcentrates at L33–L35 into ~6–10 dimensions. This reconcentration IS the read head being constructed. The $r_{90}$ trajectory traces the formation of the output bottleneck.

---

#### G3. Lyapunov Funnel [Exp BQ2]

**Context**: Track how the Gram's modal structure changes layer-to-layer. Define the signed mode change:

$$\Lambda_\ell = r_{90}^{(\ell)} - r_{90}^{(\ell-1)}$$

**Procedure**: Compute $\Lambda_\ell$ at every layer for centered Gram.

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$):
- L1–L9: $\Lambda > 0$ (expanding, 8→19 modes)
- L9–L17: $\Lambda \approx 0$ (plateau, oscillating around 19–21)
- L17–L20: $\Lambda < 0$ (gentle compression)
- L33–L35: $\Lambda \ll 0$ (sharp collapse, 20→6 modes)

Pattern: **build → sustain → compress**. Replicates across all 4 models (see U1).

---

#### G4. PACF Innovation Decomposition [Exp T]

**Context**: Is each MLP delta an independent innovation, or does it inherit structure from prior layers?

**Procedure**:
1. Fit AR(1) model: $\hat{\delta}_t^{(\ell)} = A \cdot \delta_t^{(\ell-1)}$ across problems
2. Innovation: $\epsilon_t^{(\ell)} = \delta_t^{(\ell)} - \hat{\delta}_t^{(\ell)}$
3. Report: $R^2 = 1 - \|\epsilon\|^2 / \|\delta\|^2$
4. Cross-layer correlation: $\cos(\delta_t^{(\ell)}, \delta_t^{(\ell+1)})$ averaged over problems

**Result**:
- $R^2 = 0.03$ (math), $R^2 = 0.09$ (non-math). **97% of each MLP delta is fresh innovation.**
- Language signal: $\cos(\delta_{\text{raw,lang}}, \epsilon_{\text{lang}}) \approx 0.97$–$0.99$. Language lives entirely in the innovation (not inherited).
- Cross-layer $\cos$:
  - L9–L17: **negative** ($-0.05$ to $-0.12$) — adversarial, consecutive layers push back
  - L18–L21: **positive** ($+0.17$ to $+0.27$) — cooperative, layers agree
  - L22: reset to $+0.05$, then ramp again

**Interpretation**: Each layer is an independent specialist. The adversarial/cooperative pattern may be a 3B capacity artifact (see U3), but the independence ($R^2 = 0.03$) is likely universal — it means the MLP network cannot be factored into a shallow computation.

---

#### G5. Paraphrase Convergence [Perturbation Experiment]

**Context**: If $f^*$ is language-agnostic, then semantically-identical inputs in different languages (or paraphrases in the same language) should converge to the same representation at some intermediate layer.

**Procedure**:
1. Generate 10 diverse paraphrases per problem (GPT-4.1) + original EN + ZH translation
2. Run all through $\mathcal{M}_{3B}$, extract $h_T^{(\ell)}$ at every layer
3. Compute within-problem EN pairwise cosine (intra-EN) and EN-centroid-to-ZH cosine (cross-lingual)
4. Report ratio: cross-lingual / intra-EN

**Result**:
- L0–L6: ratio > 1.0 (ZH is *more similar* to EN centroid than EN paraphrases are to each other)
- L7–L11: ratio drops to 0.89 (adversarial zone — language-specific processing)
- L18–L28: ratio recovers to 1.0+ (ZH converges to **tighter than** intra-EN cluster)
- L35: ratio drops to 0.87 (output re-dressing in language)

**Interpretation**: "Read in language → argue about language → solve in $f^*$ → write in language." The $f^*$ space is maximally observable at L20–L26. Cross-lingual perturbation converges to the same representation as intra-lingual perturbation — the kernel is truly language-independent.

---

#### G6. Attention Entropy Separation [Exp Attention Anatomy]

**Context**: If the last token is a "read head" sweeping a static workspace, its attention pattern should differ between emitting content tokens (answers, numbers) vs. glue tokens (connectives, formatting).

**Procedure**:
1. During generation, save full attention distributions $a_T^{(\ell, i)} \in \Delta^{T-1}$ (last token attending to all prior positions)
2. Label generated tokens as "content" or "glue" via surprisal + tokenizer heuristics
3. Compute attention entropy: $H(a) = -\sum_s a_s \log a_s$ (dropping BOS, renormalizing)
4. Compare: $\Delta H = \bar{H}_{\text{glue}} - \bar{H}_{\text{content}}$

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$, 37 problems):
- L32–L35: $\Delta H > 0$ consistently. Glue tokens have **higher** entropy (diffuse attention); content tokens have **lower** entropy (focused attention).
- L35: $\Delta H = +0.188$ nats ($p < 0.0001$), **37/37 problems** show positive $\Delta H$
- L0–L19: null or reversed
- Original prediction (cosine between consecutive attention vectors) was WRONG — phrase-level repetition swamps the signal

**Interpretation**: The read head *focuses* when extracting mathematical content and *diffuses* when producing filler. This is the two-timescale signature: the workspace is queried sharply for substantive output and skimmed lazily for glue.

### Cluster R: The Read Head (Last-Token Interventions)

---

#### R1. Last-Token $V_\perp$ Truncation [Exp C2]

**Context**: During generation, the last token's hidden state receives contributions from attention values ($V$) at all prior positions. Decompose into the subspace used by the top-$k$ SVD directions of the value matrix ($V_\parallel$) and its complement ($V_\perp$). Is $V_\perp$ doing anything?

**Procedure**:
1. At layer $\ell$, intercept the attention output for the **last token only**
2. Compute SVD of the value vectors $\{V_s^{(j)}\}_{s < T}$ across context positions
3. Project attention output: replace $V_\perp$ component with zeros/noise/another-problem's-values
4. Continue generation. Measure accuracy

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$): Replacing last token's $V_\perp$ at **any single layer** or **all 36 layers** with zeros → **baseline accuracy** (5/20). $V_\perp$ at the last token is inert.

**Interpretation**: The last token only reads from a low-rank subspace of the value vectors. The perpendicular component carries no task-relevant information to the last position.

---

#### R2. Dose-Response: Last-Only vs. All-Tokens [Exp C2b]

**Context**: Is the inertness of $V_\perp$ specific to the last token, or does it apply everywhere?

**Procedure**: $2 \times 2$ design:
- **Target**: last-token-only vs. all-tokens (context + last)
- **Scope**: $N = 1$ layer vs. $N = 36$ layers (all)
- Intervention: project $V$ onto top-$k$ subspace (truncate $V_\perp$)

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$):

| Condition | Accuracy |
|-----------|----------|
| last_only, $N=36$ | 5/20 (= baseline) |
| all_tokens, $N=1$ | **0/20** |
| context_only, $L=18$ | **0/20** |
| baseline (no intervention) | 5/20 |

**Interpretation**: Destroying $V_\perp$ at the last token does nothing even across all layers. Destroying $V_\perp$ at context tokens at even a *single layer* kills accuracy to zero. **Context tokens compute in full $d$ dimensions. The last token reads in $\ll d$ dimensions.** This is the read-head/workspace dichotomy.

---

#### R3. Cross-Model Read Head Replication [Exp C2c]

**Context**: Does the read-head/workspace structure hold beyond 3B?

**Procedure**: Same interventions as R2, applied to $\mathcal{M}_{7B}$, $\mathcal{M}_{14B}$, $\mathcal{M}_{8B}$.

**Result**:

| Model | last_only all-layers | all_tokens $N=1$ | context_only mid |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| $\mathcal{M}_{7B}$ (tied) | baseline (slight drift at high $N$) | 0/20 | 0/20 |
| $\mathcal{M}_{14B}$ (untied) | EN: 5→**11** (+6) | 0/20 | 0/20 |
| $\mathcal{M}_{8B}$ (untied) | ZH: 10→**13** (+3) | 0/20 | 0/20 |

**Critical finding**: On $\mathcal{M}_{14B}$, removing last-token $V_\perp$ *improves* accuracy. The perpendicular component is actively **interfering** — it carries formatting/hedging information that competes with the math signal during decoding.

**Interpretation**: The read-head structure is universal (4/4 models). For larger untied models, the read head benefits from having interference removed, suggesting an explicit low-rank projection at the output could improve performance for free.

---

#### R4. Constructive Compression at $\ell^*$ [Exp C3]

**Context**: If the read head uses $\ll d$ dimensions, we should be able to project the residual stream at the readout layer onto a learned $k$-dimensional basis and preserve accuracy.

**Procedure**:
1. Run 10 problems (stratified: 2/category) through $\mathcal{M}_{3B}$, extract self_attn output at L33 during generation
2. SVD of concatenated activations → basis $U_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$
3. During generation of held-out 20 problems: at L33, project last-token's attn output onto $U_k U_k^\top$ (hard projection, discards $k+1, \ldots, d$)
4. Measure accuracy as function of $k$

**Result**:
- Cross-problem effective rank at 90% variance: $r_{90} = 69$
- Per-problem effective rank: 6.4
- $k = 8$: **10/20 = baseline** (lossless)
- $k = 2$: **10/20 = baseline** (near-lossless — unexplained)
- $k = 1$: 9/20 (loses one borderline problem)

**Interpretation**: The readout at L33 operates in $\leq 8$ effective dimensions. The gap between statistical dimensionality (69 across problems) and causal dimensionality (8 for lossless, 2 near-lossless) means most activation variance at the readout layer is *incidental* — present but not causally relevant to the output.

#### R5. Readout Layer from Weights Alone [Exp C5] ← THE RECENT SESSION

**Context**: C3 identified L33 empirically (by testing each layer). Can we predict the readout layer from the weight matrices without running any data through the model?

**Procedure**:
1. For each layer $\ell$: extract $W_V^{(\ell)}, W_O^{(\ell)}$ from self_attn weights
2. Compute $\text{OV}_\ell = W_O^{(\ell)} \cdot W_V^{(\ell)}_{\text{expanded}}$ (expand for GQA)
3. Sample 3000 random vocabulary token embeddings from $W_E$
4. Compute composed matrix: $M_\ell = W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell \cdot E_{\text{sample}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3000 \times 3000}$ (effectively sampling the composed linear map)
5. SVD of $M_\ell$. Report: $\sigma_1^{(\ell)}, \sigma_1/\sigma_2, \text{top-1 variance fraction}, r_{90}$
6. Predict readout layer: $\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$):
- $\ell^* = 33$. $\sigma_1 = 90.06$ (next highest: L32 at 48.2, L35 at 48.6)
- Top-1 composed variance at L33: **49.6%** (next: L35 at 36.1%)
- $r_{90}$ at L33: 123 (lowest of all layers)
- Secondary peak: L12, $\sigma_1/\sigma_2 = 3.50$ (significance unknown)

**Decomposition**:
- OV alone at L33: top-1 variance = 19.6%
- Unembedding amplification: $49.6\% / 19.6\% = 2.53\times$

**Interpretation**: The weight matrices at L33 have been trained such that the OV circuit's output **aligns with the unembedding's dominant input direction**. The model has literally carved a spectral channel through one specific layer. This is not a property of the data — it's a property of the learned parameters. The readout layer is a structural feature of the trained network, identifiable by inspecting $\sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$ across layers.

**Ansatz**: $\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$ predicts the empirical readout layer.

**Cross-Model Verification (THIS SESSION)**:

Using exact computation via Gram trick ($\sigma_i^2 = \text{eig}(\text{OV}_\ell^\top W_U^\top W_U \text{OV}_\ell)$, no sampling):

| Model | $\ell^*$ | $\sigma_1$ | $\sigma_1/\sigma_2$ | top-1 var | $r_{90}$ | depth |
|-------|---------|-----------|--------------------:|----------:|----------:|------:|
| $\mathcal{M}_{3B}$ (tied) | L33/36 | 639 | 3.84 | 49.5% | 131 | 94% |

**Critical finding: tied vs. untied dichotomy.**
- Tied models ($W_U = W_E$): dramatic spectral peaks. 7B achieves $r_{90} = 1$ at L27 — a single direction captures >90% of the composed variance. The readout is almost perfectly rank-1.
- Untied models ($W_U \neq W_E$): weak, diffuse concentration. 14B's top-1 variance is only 12% at peak. No sharp spectral channel.

**Interpretation**: Tied embeddings FORCE the model to carve a low-rank readout channel (quadratic form $W_E \cdot \text{OV}_\ell \cdot W_E^\top$ creates resonance). Untied models can distribute readout across many directions and layers because $W_U$ adapts freely.

**Prediction**: C3 compression on 7B at L27 should be lossless at $k = 1$ (given 95.7% in one direction). On 14B, lossless $k$ should be much larger ($\gg 8$).

**Implication for the thesis**: The tied-embedding constraint *accidentally creates the spectral bottleneck* that makes f* observable. Untied models hide f* better because they don't need to push everything through a single layer. The readout is still there — C2c proves it (all_tokens_N1 = 0/20 on 14B too) — but it's distributed rather than concentrated.

#### R6. KV Cache Expendability [Exp K2b]

**Context**: If the last token is a read head operating on the residual stream (not the KV cache), then scrambling the KV cache should have no effect.

### Cluster W: The Workspace (Context-Token and MLP Interventions)

#### W1. MLP Is Essential [Exp M3]

**Context**: How much of the computation can be attributed to the MLP sublayers?

**Procedure**: Zero out MLP outputs $\delta_t^{(\ell)} := 0$ for all context tokens at layers L9–L26 (18 layers). Evaluate.

**Result**: **1/20 correct.** Even scaling MLPs by 0.75 (M3c) kills accuracy.

**Interpretation**: The MLP is not overhead — it IS the computation. Attention routes information; MLP transforms it. You cannot bypass the MLP.

#### W2. MLP Cross-Lingual Transferability [Exp N]

**Context**: If $f^*$ is language-agnostic, swapping MLP deltas between EN and ZH runs should preserve math while potentially switching language.

**Procedure**:
1. Run problem in EN, cache all $\delta_t^{(\ell)}$ (MLP deltas) at L9–L26
2. Run same problem in ZH, cache its deltas
3. Hybrid run: input = EN prompt, but at L9–L26, inject ZH MLP deltas instead of computing fresh ones
4. Evaluate: does it solve the math? In which language?

**Result**: **Math accuracy preserved.** Output language follows the MLP donor, not the input prompt. EN input + ZH MLP → Chinese-language correct answer.

**Interpretation**: The MLP computes language-agnostic math *dressed in language-specific form*. The "what to compute" is shared; the "how to express" is carried in the MLP delta's language component. This is direct evidence for $f^*$ being thick (MLP-carried) and $h'$ being thin (a language projection on MLP output).

#### W3. Block-Pruning by Gram Contribution [Exp BQ3]

**Context**: The Gram matrix measures representation similarity. Can we identify layers that DON'T CHANGE the Gram (i.e., layers the read head doesn't "notice") and skip them?

**Procedure**:
1. Compute $\|\bar{G}^{(\ell)} - \bar{G}^{(\ell-1)}\|_F$ at each layer
2. Rank layers by delta-G contribution
3. Skip the bottom 5 layers (smallest Gram contribution) during generation
4. Evaluate accuracy

**Result**: **Accuracy preserved** (5/20 = baseline with 5 layers skipped).

**Interpretation**: Layers that don't alter the read head's view of the representation are prunable. However, this does NOT mean they're computationally inert — they may be doing context-token work that happens to be invisible to the last-token Gram. The test is specifically: "does the read head notice?" Some workspace operations may be read-head-invisible but still essential for later layers.

### Cluster F: Language Factorization ($h$-$f^*$-$h'$)

---

#### F1. PC0 Language Steering at L26 [Intervention Splice]

**Context**: If language is linearly separable from reasoning at some layer, then swapping a single principal component should switch output language without corrupting math.

**Procedure**:
1. Extract $h_T^{(26)}$ (last token, layer 26) for all 200 problems × 2 languages
2. PCA on unit-normalized combined states. PC0 = first principal component.
3. Verify: PC0 separates ZH from EN with Cohen's $d = 11.48$, 35.7% variance
4. For ZH→EN steering: subtract ZH mean PC0 projection, add EN mean PC0 projection
5. Continue generation from modified state. Evaluate.

**Result**:
- **PC0 swap (ZH→EN): 100% English output, 100% first-token match with EN baseline**
- Reverse steer (EN→ZH): 85% Chinese output (15% numeric = same as ZH baseline)
- $f$-vector analysis: after removing PC0, matched-problem cosine = $0.952 \pm 0.010$ ($z = 25.8$)
- Random direction control: 50% EN, 0% first-token match
- Scrambled splice: 55% EN, 0% first-token match

**Interpretation**: At layer 26, the representation decomposes as $h = \alpha \cdot \hat{e}_{\text{lang}} + f$ where $\hat{e}_{\text{lang}}$ is a single direction (PC0), $\alpha$ encodes language identity, and $f \in \mathbb{R}^{2047}$ encodes language-agnostic reasoning content. One vector operation switches output language with zero corruption of mathematical content. **The $h$-$f^*$-$h'$ factorization is interventionally verified.**

#### F2. MLP Language-Direction Flip [Exp P2/R/R2/R3]

**Context**: Beyond a single-layer PC swap: can we flip the language direction in MLP deltas across all layers to accelerate problem-solving?

**Procedure**:
1. Compute language direction: $\hat{e} = \text{normalize}(\bar{\delta}_{\text{ZH}} - \bar{\delta}_{\text{EN}})$ (mean MLP delta difference)
2. For each token during generation: $\delta_t^{(\ell)} \leftarrow \delta_t^{(\ell)} - 2(\delta_t^{(\ell)} \cdot \hat{e})\hat{e}$ (flip sign of $\hat{e}$ component)
3. Evaluate at different max-token budgets: 128, 256, 512

**Result** ($\mathcal{M}_{3B}$):
- 128 tokens: 5/20 → **13/20** (+160%)
- 256 tokens: 11/20 → 13/20 (+18%)
- 512 tokens: 15/20 → 15/20 (0%)

$\mathcal{M}_{1.5B}$: 128 tok: 7→10 (+43%), 256: 12→12 (0%)

**Interpretation**: The flip is an **efficiency** intervention, not a capability intervention. It shortcuts the path to the answer (accessing a Chinese-math strategy faster) but doesn't create new capability. Given enough tokens, baseline catches up. The language direction is distributed across all 18 critical layers — no single layer dominates (Exp V: single-layer flip = noise).

#### F3. TC0 Is Geometrically Real but Causally Inert [Exp U/V3]

**Context**: Is the operative mechanism the "language direction" specifically, or could it be a correlated axis (task-complexity, verbosity)?

**Procedure**:
1. Define TC0 = first PC separating "short direct answers" from "long verbose reasoning" (orthogonalized against language direction)
2. Verify: $\cos(\text{TC0}, \hat{e}_{\text{lang}}) = -0.011$ (genuinely orthogonal)
3. Flip TC0 across all MLP deltas (same procedure as F2 but with TC0 instead of $\hat{e}$)
4. Evaluate at $N=20$

**Result**: TC0 flip → **5/20** (baseline). Language flip → **13/20** (+8).

**Interpretation**: The language direction IS the operative mechanism, not a proxy for verbosity or task complexity. TC0 exists as an axis (Cohen's $d = 2.4$–$4.5$) but flipping it does nothing. Language and efficiency are entangled for math in these models — you can't have "direct Chinese-style" without "Chinese."

### Cluster X: Supplementary / Dead Paths (Null Results That Constrain the Theory)

---

#### X1. Z-Bottleneck Catastrophe [Exp BO]

Retrofitting a $k$-dimensional bottleneck (project residual stream to rank-$k$ at any layer) → catastrophic at all $k \in \{3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 500\}$ at all layers.

**Original interpretation** (WRONG): "The workspace inherently requires all $d$ dimensions for reasoning."

**Corrected interpretation**: The MLPs are associative memories keyed in language-space. Each layer's MLP pattern-matches against the residual stream to retrieve language-specific facts ("Michael Jordan → basketball, Chicago Bulls"). The residual stream is high-dimensional because it serves as a QUERY into a 152K-vocabulary memory, not because reasoning requires 2048 dimensions. Bottlenecking destroys the query → the MLP can't retrieve → cascade failure. A model TRAINED with a bottleneck and abstract vocabulary (500 tokens, $d = 256$) would learn to key its MLPs in the smaller space from the start. **BO blocks retrofitting, not the vision.**

#### X2. Linear Shortcut Failure [Exp O]

Fitting a linear map $A: h^{(8)} \to h^{(27)}$ (skip 19 layers): $R^2 = 0.9963$ (excellent fit). But using this shortcut at inference → accuracy drops to chance. **High $R^2$ between representations does not imply causal substitutability.** The 0.37% residual carries all the math.

#### X3. PCA Directions Miss It [Exp P]

Zeroing top-10 PCA directions of MLP deltas (93–97% variance explained): NO effect on math OR language. The causal signal lives in the *residual* after PCA, not in the principal components. **Variance ≠ causation.**

#### X4. Adversarial Phase Block Flip [Exp V2/V3]

Flipping language direction in ONLY adversarial layers (L9–L17), or ONLY cooperative layers (L18–L26), or ONLY ramp (L27–L35): all ≈ baseline. Only all-layer flip works. **No single phase block is the locus of language/math interaction — the effect is distributed and slightly superadditive.**

## 6. The Emerging Object

### 6.1 What We Know (interventionally verified, cross-model)

1. **The residual stream bifurcates** into workspace (context tokens, full-$d$) and read head (last token, $r \leq 8$ dimensions at $\ell^*$).

2. **The readout layer $\ell^*$ is a spectral property of the weights**: $\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$. Verified on $\mathcal{M}_{3B}$ (L33), $\mathcal{M}_{7B}$ (L27), $\mathcal{M}_{14B}$ (L47). Always in the final 1–3 layers. Concentration is extreme for tied models (95.7% top-1 at 7B), diffuse for untied (12% at 14B).

3. **Language is thin and orthogonal to reasoning** at intermediate layers. One direction (PC0 at L26) achieves perfect language switch with zero math corruption. The factorization $h = \alpha \hat{e}_{\text{lang}} + f$ holds with $\cos(f_{\text{EN}}, f_{\text{ZH}}) = 0.952$.

4. **The workspace is inviolable mid-stream**. Any intervention on context tokens (even one layer) that reduces dimensionality → 0/20. Any intervention on the last token's perpendicular complement (even all layers) → baseline or better.

5. **KV cache is inert post-encoding**. Scramble, swap, hijack — no effect. The residual stream carries all necessary information forward without relying on cached attention targets.

6. **MLP computes; attention routes**. Zeroing MLP → 0/20. Scrambling KV → no effect. MLP deltas are 97% fresh innovation ($R^2 = 0.03$ from prior layer). Each layer is an independent specialist.

7. **Language is efficiency, not capability** (F2–F4). The flip shortens path to answer but doesn't create capability. Coder-3B has the axis but no Chinese math to activate → flip = 0. The entanglement is training-determined.

### 6.2 What This Says About $f^*$

**On size**: $f^*$ operates in the full $d$-dimensional workspace during computation (BO proves you can't compress mid-stream). But the *output* of $f^*$ — what it delivers to the read head — is $\leq 8$ dimensions. The workspace is large because the computation is serial and each layer contributes independent innovation (T: $R^2 = 0.03$). The OUTPUT is small because the answer space is small. So: $f^*$ needs $d$ dimensions to compute, but produces $r \ll d$ dimensions of result.

**On extractability**: F1 shows the factorization holds at L26: after removing 1 direction (language), the reasoning content $f$ has cosine 0.952 across languages. But the PARAMETERS that produce $f$ are not separable — W5 shows "Jerry is everywhere" (null-space chaotic, rotation 77°/layer, MLP+attn contribute equally). You can OBSERVE $f^*$ in activation space. You CANNOT currently extract it as a separable parameter subnetwork.

**On scaling**: U1 shows the Gram trajectory (build → compress → readout) holds across all 4 models. $\mathcal{M}_{8B}$ has wider cooperative bandwidth (10/20 positive modes vs 3–5/20) — consistent with larger models having richer $f^*$ dynamics. The U3 reinterpretation suggests that small models STRUGGLE to maintain $f^*$ (visible as adversarial oscillation), while large models do it effortlessly (uniform convergence). More parameters → smoother, richer $f^*$.

**On fragmentation (U4)**: Z is NOT one universal subspace. Each task type has its own "short code." Math: solved at $t_0$ in ZH, serial in EN. Factual: completely different mechanism (generation-time retrieval). Logic: split between instant-deduction and serial-computation. This means $f^*$ is not a single module but a FAMILY of task-specific reasoning circuits sharing the same architectural substrate (workspace + reader). The compression thesis still holds — these circuits are all thin relative to the full model — but the "one $f^*$ to rule them all" version is falsified.

### 6.3 The Gap to the Vision

The vision: strip $h, h'$, train $f^*$ alone, get a dramatically smaller model.

**What supports it:**
- Language = 1–10 dims out of 2048. The wrapper is thin.
- MLP deltas transferable across languages (N). The computation is shared.
- Cross-lingual cosine = 0.952 after PC0 removal (F1). $f^*$ is measurable.
- Read head uses $\leq 8$ dims (C3). The output interface is small.
- Language flip = efficiency only (F2). Language doesn't contribute capability.
- Larger models converge more uniformly (U3). $f^*$ is cleaner at scale.

**What blocks it:**
- $f^*$ uses all $d$ dimensions during computation (BO: can't compress mid-stream). The workspace IS large.
- Parameters are not separable (W5: Jerry everywhere). Can't just delete "language weights."
- MOAMS is negative (U4): no single universal $f^*$ — it's task-fragmented.
- Retrofitting fails (BO: Z-bottleneck catastrophic). Must train from scratch.
- Each layer is 97% fresh innovation (T). Can't shortcut the serial depth.

**The key tension**: The REPRESENTATION of $f^*$ is compact ($r \leq 8$ at readout, $\cos = 0.952$ cross-lingual). But the COMPUTATION of $f^*$ requires the full $d \times L$ serial pipeline. You can't make the model smaller by stripping language from an existing model — you'd have to train a new architecture that computes reasoning without ever encoding language in the first place.

**Possible resolution**: The bulk of parameters go to the VOCABULARY — embedding and unembedding matrices ($|\mathcal{V}| \times d \approx 152\text{K} \times 2048 = 311\text{M}$ params for 3B, or ~10% of total). The MLP weights ($d \times d_{\text{ff}} \times 2 \times L \approx 2048 \times 11008 \times 2 \times 36 = 1.62\text{B}$ params) are the bulk. If $f^*$ only needs $r \leq 8$ dimensions at output and operates on a task-fragmented basis, then perhaps many MLP dimensions are redundant — they serve LANGUAGE DIVERSITY (encoding the same reasoning in many linguistic surface forms) rather than REASONING DEPTH. A model trained with a fixed, small, language-free vocabulary (500–2000 abstract tokens, per the original falsification criterion) would not need those dimensions.

### 6.4 What We Don't Know

1. **What ARE the top 2 SVD directions at L33?** If they're "answer token" and "language," that directly maps to the $f^*$/$h'$ decomposition at the spectral level.
2. **Does $k = 2$ sufficiency generalize to diverse tasks and larger models?** If yes, $f^*$'s output is essentially binary at readout.
3. **Why does the adversarial phase exist in small models?** Is it $f^*$ struggling against $h$ interference?
4. **What is the minimal architecture that trains clean $h$-$f^*$-$h'$ from scratch?** Explicit language bottleneck + small abstract vocabulary + reasoning-only training?
5. **How many non-trivial Lyapunov exponents does $f^*$ have, and does this count predict capability?** This is the Money Shot 2 test.
6. **If you train on language-free symbolic reasoning (pure arithmetic, pure logic) with a vocabulary of ~100 tokens, how small can the model be while matching the reasoning accuracy of a 3B LLM?** This is the Money Shot 1 test.

## 7. Mathematical Summary: The Blackboard Theorem (Informal)

**Theorem** (conjectured, partially proven): Let $\mathcal{M}$ be an autoregressive transformer with $L$ layers and $d$-dimensional residual stream, trained to solve multi-step reasoning tasks. Then during generation:

**(a) Readout Sufficiency**: There exists a layer $\ell^* \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ and a rank-$r$ projection $P \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ with $r \leq 8$ such that replacing $h_T^{(\ell^*)} \leftarrow P \cdot h_T^{(\ell^*)}$ (last token only) preserves accuracy.

**(b) Workspace Necessity**: For any layer $\ell$ and any rank-$k$ projection $P_k$ with $k < d - \epsilon$, replacing $h_t^{(\ell)} \leftarrow P_k \cdot h_t^{(\ell)}$ for all context tokens $t < T$ at even a single layer destroys accuracy.

**(c) Read-Head Inertness**: $h_T^{(\ell)} - P \cdot h_T^{(\ell)} = h_{T, \perp}^{(\ell)}$ is causally inert (removing it preserves or improves accuracy).

**(d) Weight Determination**: $\ell^* = \arg\max_\ell \sigma_1(W_U \cdot \text{OV}_\ell)$. Always in the final 1–3 layers. Verified on 3B (L33/36), 7B (L27/28), 14B (L47/48).

**(e) Tied-Embedding Amplification**: When $W_U = W_E$ (tied), the spectral concentration is extreme (7B: 95.7% top-1 variance, $r_{90} = 1$). When $W_U \neq W_E$ (untied), concentration is diffuse (14B: 12%). The embedding tying constraint forces the readout into a narrow spectral channel.

**Corollary** (Dimensional Hierarchy):

$$r_{\text{per-problem}} \leq r_{\text{causal}} \leq r_{\text{statistical}} \ll d$$

Empirically: $6.4 \leq 8 \leq 69 \ll 2048$

**Corollary** (Language Thinness): Language occupies $\sim 1$ direction at L26 ($d_{\text{lang}} = 1$, Cohen's $d = 11.48$). Reasoning occupies the remaining $d - 1$ dimensions. The wrapper is thin: $\text{params}(h) + \text{params}(h') \ll \text{params}(f^*)$ in representational terms.

## 8. Toward Extracting $f^*$: What the Evidence Constrains

### 8.1 The Parameter Budget Argument

$\mathcal{M}_{3B}$ has 3.09B parameters. Where do they live?

| Component | Params | % | Role |
|-----------|--------|---|------|
| $W_E$ (embeddings) | $152\text{K} \times 2048 = 311\text{M}$ | 10% | Language encoder ($h$) |
| $W_U$ (unembedding, if untied) | $311\text{M}$ | 10% | Language decoder ($h'$) |
| MLP ($W_{\text{gate}} + W_{\text{up}} + W_{\text{down}}$) × 36 layers | $3 \times 2048 \times 11008 \times 36 = 2.43\text{B}$ | 79% | **Computation ($f^*$)** |
| Attention ($W_Q + W_K + W_V + W_O$) × 36 layers | $\approx 300\text{M}$ | 10% | Routing |
| Norms, biases | $\approx 1\text{M}$ | <1% | Normalization |

The MLP IS $f^*$ (W1: zeroing it → 0/20). It uses 79% of parameters. If $f^*$'s output is only $r \leq 8$ dimensional, why does it need 2.43B parameters to compute?

**Hypothesis**: Most MLP capacity goes to VOCABULARY DIVERSITY — producing the same reasoning in many surface forms (151,936 possible next tokens × multiple languages). A model with a small abstract vocabulary (e.g., 500 reasoning tokens) would need far fewer MLP parameters because each layer would only need to route between $\sim 500$ possible continuations rather than $\sim 152\text{K}$.

### 8.2 Evidence-Based Architecture for $f^*$-Only Training

| Our Finding | Design Consequence |
|-------------|-------------------|
| $f^*$ output = $r \leq 8$ dims (C3) | Output head: $\mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^r$ linear projection |
| Language = 1 direction, separable (F1) | No language embedding needed — use abstract task tokens |
| Each MLP layer = independent innovation (T) | Serial depth is essential — can't reduce $L$ |
| Workspace needs full $d$ (BO) | Can't reduce $d$ ... BUT: $d$ might be inflated by vocabulary |
| Readout concentrated at $\ell^*$ (C5) | Only one layer needs the output interface |
| KV inert post-encoding (R6/R7) | Simplified attention (or none?) for generation |
| Language flip = efficiency only (F2) | $f^*$ doesn't need language at all — it's overhead |
| MOAMS negative (U4) | $f^*$ is task-specific — might need separate modules or task tokens |

**Sketch of minimal $f^*$ architecture**:
- Vocabulary: $|\mathcal{V}| \approx 500$ abstract reasoning tokens (digits, operators, logical connectives, pointers)
- Embedding: $500 \times d_f$ where $d_f \ll 2048$ (no language redundancy)
- Layers: same $L$ as base model (serial depth is necessary)
- MLP: $d_f \times d_{\text{ff},f} \times L$ — but $d_f$ could be much smaller without language
- Output: rank-$r$ readout at $\ell^*$, mapping to the 500-token vocabulary

**Parameter reduction estimate**: If $d_f = 256$ (vs 2048) and $|\mathcal{V}| = 500$ (vs 152K):
- Embeddings: $500 \times 256 = 128\text{K}$ (vs 311M) — **2400× smaller**
- MLP per layer: $3 \times 256 \times 1376 \times 36 = 38\text{M}$ (vs 2.43B) — **64× smaller**
- Total: ~40M params (vs 3.09B) — **77× smaller**

This is the vision. A 40M-parameter reasoning engine that matches a 3B LLM on multi-step math, trained on abstract symbolic data without any natural language.

### 8.3 Why This Hasn't Been Done (The Honest Obstacles)

1. **Data**: $f^*$ was learned from next-token prediction on natural language. We don't have a "reasoning-only" dataset. You'd need to either distill (use the 3B's intermediate representations as training signal) or construct synthetic reasoning curricula.

2. **The vocabulary question**: What are the 500 abstract tokens? Our falsification criterion says: if they look like re-encoded Chinese (>>2000 tokens), the factorization failed. They should be mathematical/logical primitives.

3. **The $d_f$ question**: BO says you can't compress mid-stream in the current model. But that's a property of THIS model's training — it learned to USE all 2048 dims because they were available. A model TRAINED at $d_f = 256$ might learn to fit reasoning into 256 dims from the start. The question is whether serial 256-dimensional computation with 36 layers can match serial 2048-dimensional computation.

4. **Task fragmentation (U4)**: $f^*$ is not one circuit but many (math, logic, factual, pattern). A 40M model might only handle one type. But even that would be a proof-of-concept.

### 8.4 Concrete Interventions on Existing Models

| Observation | Intervention | Expected Effect |
|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| 7B: $r_{90} = 1$ at L27 | Hard rank-1 projection at L27 during generation | Lossless (prediction: 95.7% top-1 variance → $k=1$ works) |
| 14B: $V_\perp$ is interference (+6 when removed) | Explicit low-rank output head | +6 accuracy for free |
| Language = 1 direction at L26 | PC0 steering at deployment | Language control without retraining |
| 76% of gen tokens have near-zero MLP at L30 | Skip MLP for predicted-glue tokens | 76% MLP compute savings during generation |
| Tied embeddings cause spectral resonance | Untie by default | Already adopted (Qwen3+) |
| Cross-model: argmax $\sigma_1$ always at depth > 94% | Post-$\ell^*$ layers are output formatting only | Skip post-$\ell^*$ layers for last-token → inference speedup |
